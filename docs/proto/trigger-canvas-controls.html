Candidate controls — read out of the real Trigger view
Before deciding where, decide what. These are the controls that live in the Inspector today and are touched often enough to earn canvas space. Sourced from PlayNodeInspector.svelte, trigger-node-meta.ts and the effect paramSpecs in packages/core/src/effects/impl/. One row is an assumption, not a found requirement — it is marked as such.
ControlNode kindsWhy it earns canvas spaceSource
Colour (hue / sat / bri) play · effect 49 of ~45 effects declare saturation + brightness, 40 declare hue. It is the single most-shared triplet in the library and the one you nudge while watching the kit. grep of impl/*.ts paramSpec
Decay / Life play · effect How long a hit hangs. decayMs ×9, life/lifeMs/lifeBeats ×7 — and it is the param you tune against a groove, not against a number. radial-wash, whole-drum, confetti-burst
Speed play · effect 30 effects declare it; units differ (°/s, mm/ms, rev/beat) but the gesture is identical. comet-trails, chase-bands, radial-wash
Chance % chance Already printed on the card sub line (kindSummary → "45%") — reading it there but editing it three panels away is the exact friction this explores. trigger-node-meta.ts
Delay time delay Same story: the card already says 120ms or 1/8. kindSummary case 'delay'
Bypass modifier A single boolean with a whole-node visual consequence (the card already dims to 0.55 opacity). TriggerNode.svelte .bypassed
Play mode / Layer play · effect Segmented, 3 values, set-once-then-rarely. Weak candidate — listed so the "no" is on record. MODE_OPTS, store.buses
Fire (test one hit) ASSUMED trigger · play There is no fire-to-test affordance on main today — no fireNode, no mute, no solo anywhere in the trigger store. Included because the brief names it, and because every option below is more valuable if you can hear the change. Treat as a proposal, not a port. assumed — brief only
Not candidates. Scope + target (a dropdown of every drum · hoop), preset select, and the modulation-target rows — those already have their place and their space.
Shared constraint. Whatever lands on the canvas must survive being zoomed out. Options below are drawn at 100%; a real pass has to answer "what happens at 40%".
1
Face Params — the card grows the controls
Two or three params are promoted onto the node card itself, in the footer slot the card already has for modulation rows. Always visible, no selection required, no new surface — the graph becomes a rack of small devices you read and adjust in place.
Forced from: the app's own NodeCard footer + an Ableton device rack — the control lives on the device.
Wins — zero-click editing, and the graph shows its settings without you selecting anything. Reuses a footer mechanism that already ships.
Costs — every card gets taller, so the graph gets sparser and zoom-out gets worse. Which 2 params get promoted is per-effect config you now have to author or infer.
Open — do promoted params also stay in the Inspector (two truths on screen), or vanish from it?
2
Satellite — a contextual cluster that flies to the selection
Cards stay exactly as they ship. Selecting a node floats a small cluster beside it with that node's hot params; it follows the selection and dissolves on canvas click. Nothing is permanently on the canvas, so nothing is permanently in the way.
Forced from: Figma's floating selection toolbar — chrome that exists only while something is selected.
Wins — card density untouched; the cluster can be as rich as it likes because it is temporary. Reads instantly as "belongs to this node".
Costs — occludes whatever is behind it (here it deliberately overlaps a wire). Needs collision logic near the canvas edges. You cannot compare two nodes' values at once.
Open — does it also open on hover (fast, twitchy) or only on click (calm, one extra gesture)?
3
Wire Tap — the control rides the connection
The params that describe flow between nodes — chance, delay, modifier bypass — stop being node properties on the canvas and become handles on the wire itself. Drag the pill left/right to scrub the value; click it to open in place. The graph stops being cards-with-settings and starts being a signal path with inline attenuators.
Forced from: Max/MSP patch cords + Blender's reroute nodes — the edge is a first-class editable object.
Wins — the truest picture of what the graph does: "60% of the time, 120 ms later". Costs the cards nothing. Scales with graph size instead of node count.
Costs — only fits gating/timing params. Chance and Delay are nodes today, so this is a model change, not a layout change — a wire tap would have to create/edit a hidden node.
Open — this is the one option that argues with the data model. Worth it only if wire-level chance is where you actually want to author.
4
Corner HUD — one fixed panel, retargeted by selection
A single translucent panel pinned to the canvas corner. It always shows the selected node's hot controls plus Fire, and never moves — so the muscle memory is positional, not spatial. Nothing is attached to any card; the canvas stays a clean map.
Forced from: a hardware transport / DaVinci-style corner inspector — a fixed cockpit, not floating chrome.
Wins — one position to learn; controls can be big enough to hit while looking at the kit, not the screen. Survives zoom-out completely (it is not in canvas space).
Costs — it is a small Inspector in a corner, which is close to admitting the Inspector was right. Permanently eats a canvas corner. No node→control adjacency at all.
Open — if this wins, the honest question is whether it replaces the Inspector rather than joining it (see the drawer prototype).
