0
Baseline — today's docked Node Editor
What ships now (NodeEditor.svelte + Splitter): a permanent full-height panel beside the canvas, two tabs (Add / Inspector), user-resizable, never closes. Selecting a node flips the tab to Inspector. Included so every option below is judged against the thing it would replace, not against a memory of it.
Reference point: the app as merged. Not an option — the control.
The complaint this prototype answers — the panel is always there whether or not you are editing, so the canvas is permanently ~320px narrower than the window.
The thing it gets right — no motion, no state, no surprise. The Add palette and the Inspector are one object with one position.
1
Auto Overlay — selection opens it, canvas click dismisses it
Closed means gone: full canvas width, nothing on the right edge. Click a node and the panel slides over the canvas (it does not reflow — the graph never moves under your cursor). Click empty canvas, or press Escape, and it slides away. Selection is the open state.
Forced from: a mobile sheet / Gmail's contextual side panel — summoned by content, dismissed by tapping away.
Wins — the canvas is full width the whole time you are wiring. Zero chrome when you are not editing. Overlay means the graph layout never shifts, so nothing jumps under the pointer.
Costs — it covers the right third of the graph, which is exactly where a node you just wired often sits. "Select to inspect" and "select to multi-drag" now fight for the same gesture.
Try — click a node, then click empty canvas. Then click a node and press Escape.
2
Peek Rail — push when pinned, overlay when not, never fully gone
Closed collapses to a 44px rail that still tells you what is selected (its icon chip + name, vertical). Click the rail to open. The pin decides the mechanic: pinned pushes the canvas (nothing is ever hidden, the graph reflows), unpinned overlays it and auto-closes when you click away.
Forced from: VS Code's activity bar + Linear's issue panel — a persistent edge that is a control, not just a wall.
Wins — the only option where "what is selected" survives closing the panel. The pin gives you both mechanics without a settings screen. Pinned-push guarantees no node is ever occluded.
Costs — the most state of the four (open × pinned = 4 combinations to design and remember). The rail is a permanent 44px tax. Push means the graph slides when you open it.
Try — toggle the pin, then select a node. Compare how the canvas behaves in each mode.
3
Focus Panel — selection does nothing; you summon it deliberately
Selecting a node selects a node. To edit, you ask: double-click, or press Enter. Then a wide (460px) panel comes over a dimmed canvas — a deliberate mode with room for the full effect inspector, not a squeezed column. Escape or a click on the scrim leaves the mode.
Forced from: Notion's peek modal / a Figma "open in full" — editing is a mode you enter, not a side effect of clicking.
Wins — select and edit stop competing, so click-select stays cheap and fast. The panel can be wide enough for a real effect inspector (which is the other half of this initiative). Scrim makes the mode unmistakable.
Costs — a whole extra gesture on the most common task. The scrim blocks the canvas, so you cannot tweak a param and immediately re-wire. Worst option for "nudge decay while the drummer plays".
Try — single-click a node (nothing opens), then double-click it or press Enter.
